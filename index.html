Customer Service Decision Trees

  • Real work
  • In daily use
  • Claude Code

I built a production workflow that turns the product team's Excel decision trees into SCORM 2004 packages. Customer service agents click through these trees step by step during calls, and about 300 of them use them every day.

Sample content: The trees in these images are test trees made for privacy. The structure and design are the same as in the real documents.

Problem
Customer service agents needed to reach process documents during the call. The content came from the product teams. There was no repeatable way to turn it quickly into an interactive format that works in e-Nocta (HepsiInstitute), the company LMS.
My role
I built the project from start to finish with Claude Code. The text of the trees comes from the product team. I designed the structure that turns this text into an interactive document.
Process
  1. I split the way a customer service agent thinks during a call into four steps: the product, the customer's question, branching by situation, and the sentence to say to the customer.
  2. I designed the interface: a "Your path" panel, back buttons, and a "Tell the customer" box that highlights variable fields such as dates.
  3. I set up a 3-agent structure in Claude Code: a main session (orchestrator) that runs the workflow, a diagram reader sub-agent, and an independent quality check sub-agent that cannot change files. I also added 5 custom skills and a validation hook that runs every time a file is written.
  4. I put three approval steps in the workflow: draft, appearance and package. The text read to the customer is taken from Excel character by character. If a single character is different, publishing stops.
Challenge
It took about a week to get Claude Code to produce a SCORM package that works with e-Nocta. I solved it by copying the package structure of an Articulate package that works on HepsiInstitute and fixing the version to SCORM 2004 2nd Edition.
Result
A live reference tool that about 300 customer service agents use every day during calls. Content from the product team now turns into an interactive document quickly.

Claude Code, Python, React + Tailwind, SCORM 2004, Git, e-Nocta

Customer communication simulation series

  • Real work
  • Storyline 360
  • 4 modules

A four-module series for call center employees. It teaches the "Akıllı Sohbet" (Smart Conversation) call standard with branching scenarios that are similar to real calls. Akıllı Sohbet is Hepsiburada's internal call standard; it sets the basic rules for customer calls.

The customer in the scenario is fictional. The people in the photos are from the training team.

Problem
The whole team of about 300-400 people had to learn the Akıllı Sohbet standards in the same way and through practice. Employees who had trouble with difficult, angry customers also needed a separate place to practice.
My role
I wrote the scenarios with a scenario-writing chatbot I developed. I did all of the design and development in Storyline 360 and uploaded the SCORM 2004 package of module 1 to HepsiInstitute.
Process
  1. Customer service agents first watch the Akıllı Sohbet training video, then move on to the simulations. Before the crisis module there is also the "Empati Kurarak Dinleme Teknikleri" (Empathetic Listening Techniques) video, which I made with Premiere Pro and HeyGen.
  2. Each module covers the five steps of the standard through a different communication situation. In module 1, I wrote one correct answer and two common wrong answers for each step.
  3. I added personalization with variables: the name entered at the start is used in the feedback and throughout the scenario.
  4. A wrong choice gets feedback that explains the reason based on the standard, plus a retry. A correct choice gets reinforcement and moves on to the next step.
Module 1: Akıllı Sohbet Rehberi
A shipment tracking and delivery dispute scenario. About 300-400 people, the whole customer service team, completed the module. After that, quality scores and customer satisfaction survey results improved.
Module 2: Crisis customer simulation
Assigned to the employees who were found to have trouble in difficult calls. About 100 people completed it, and quality and survey averages improved. I tracked the results with the training impact dashboard below.

Articulate Storyline 360, SCORM 2004, HepsiInstitute (LMS), scenario-writing chatbot, Premiere Pro, HeyGen

Video production tool

  • Real work
  • Pilot
  • Claude Code

A local tool that edits screen recordings with jump cuts, creates on-brand graphics and delivers the edit to Premiere Pro as editable XML. I make the storyboard and design decisions, and I add the final touches in Premiere.

Problem
Every training video needed the same manual work: cutting silences and retakes, placing transitions and on-screen text one by one, and following the brand color and font rules. This slowed the work down and made the style differ from video to video.
My role
I built the tool with Claude Code: a rules file, a workflow, 2 custom skills and scripts. I had Claude Code measure my own edits, and I turned the results into rules. I also had it create a motion library of 23 on-brand templates.
Process
  1. Analysis: The tool transcribes the recording with Whisper and finds silences and retakes.
  2. Storyboard: It shows the sections, the parts to cut and 2-4 options for each transition and on-screen text on one page. I make the choices.
  3. Design: The selected templates are adapted to the text of the video. They come to me for approval as a preview on top of the raw recording.
  4. Delivery: Transparent graphic files and FCP7 XML that I can edit in Premiere. An automatic check runs before delivery.
Challenge
Premiere did not open a project file generated directly, so I switched delivery to FCP7 XML and kept the cuts and layers editable. The company computer has no admin rights, so I ran the tool with portable Node and user-level Python. Everything runs locally, and no content leaves the computer.
Result
The repetitive part of editing became a repeatable workflow based on rules. The first video I made with the tool was the Akıllı Sohbet training video, about 6 minutes long. Learners watch this video before the simulation series.

Claude Code, Python, FFmpeg, Whisper, Playwright, GSAP, Premiere Pro

Training impact dashboard

  • In progress
  • Claude Code

A dashboard that finds training needs in quality and customer satisfaction survey data and shows the change after training in the same place. I now start training production by looking at the data, without waiting for a request.

Sample data: Team names, training matches and values are not real.

Problem
Digital training production usually started with a request from a team. A need only became visible when someone asked for it, and whether a training worked was tracked from scattered data.
My role
I built the Excel automation that regularly moves values from the quality teams into the dashboard. I am developing the dashboard in Claude Code with a four-agent setup: data, analysis, interface, and impact and quality check.
Process
  1. I set up an Excel structure that collects quality and survey values regularly, and I automated it.
  2. The agents read this data, prioritize the teams and criteria below target, and match them with existing trainings.
  3. I create content for the top need. The simulation series started this way, from a need that showed up in the data.
  4. I track the change after training on the same screen.
Result
Training production now starts from data. The results of the simulation series were tracked with this dashboard. The project is still in development.

Claude Code, Excel automation, web interface

hepsiJET onboarding video

  • Real work
  • AI avatar

An onboarding video for new hepsiJET employees, with an AI avatar as the narrator. I chose this instead of a classic video shoot. About 200 people watched the video.

Partner logos and vehicle plates are blurred.

Problem
New employees needed onboarding content about shipment types, processes and partners. Making it with a classic video shoot would have cost a lot of time and budget.
My role
I wrote the script with my own scenario-writing chatbot. I made the avatar videos in HeyGen, the voices in ElevenLabs and the scene images with Nano Banana and Midjourney. I did all of the editing in Premiere Pro.
Challenge
Lip sync is one of the riskiest points in AI avatar videos. I chose the tools with this in mind and had no sync problems with the current version of HeyGen.
Result
About 200 people watched it. I used a chatbot for the script and HeyGen for the avatar, so I saved effort and cost compared to a classic shoot.

Scenario-writing chatbot, HeyGen, ElevenLabs, Nano Banana, Midjourney, Premiere Pro
